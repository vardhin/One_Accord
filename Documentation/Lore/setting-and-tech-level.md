# Setting & Tech Level

> The texture of the world: a collapsed manuscript-clockwork society.

## Not medieval, not modern

The setting is a **collapsed manuscript-clockwork society.**

People have: lanterns, pocket watches, clock towers, bell towers, geared locks,
mills, pumps, hand-cranked gates, ledgers, wax seals, cold rooms, quarantine
tags, and possibly **early experimental steam engines**.

### Hard tech constraints

- **No rails.**
- **Steam engines are only experimental and rare** — used for pumps, forge
  bellows, drainage, or mills. Not transport.
- **No printing press yet.** The printing press is a **major milestone and a
  possible strategic future technology** (mass-copying quarantine rules,
  severance knowledge, literacy primers).

## Collapsed object meanings

Because so few people are left, **old luxury items are no longer tied to class**.
They are scavenged survival objects:

- A gold watch times fever checks.
- Noble curtains divide sickbeds.
- A silver tray holds surgical tools.
- Pocket watches, fine lanterns, noble gloves, mirrors, carriage parts, brass
  tools, books, ornate mechanisms — all just salvage now.

> The old meanings of objects have collapsed. (The sword-girl is horrified by
> this — see `sword-girl-backstory.md`.)

## Fragile timekeeping = survival infrastructure

Mechanical watches are **not quartz**. They must be tuned, cleaned, oiled,
regulated, and compared against trusted time.

- Many watches exist as salvage; **few people know how to maintain them.**
- A watchmaker / bellwright / clock keeper / mechanic / lantern-code keeper is
  **extremely valuable.**
- **Rule: objects are common; maintenance knowledge is rare.**

## Post-collapse technocracy

Knowledge is real barter, but **survival comes first.** Survivors are not fools —
they're the ones who figured out some form of survival.

Rough value hierarchy:

1. Can you survive without endangering the group?
2. Can you do something rare that keeps others alive?
3. Can you teach it safely?

**Operable** knowledge matters more than abstract education: tuning a watch,
repairing a gate spring, copying a quarantine rule, distinguishing ash quality,
reading a fever ledger, maintaining a pump, burning bodies safely, recognizing
active husks, preserving seed grain.

## Specialized islands of competence

Survivor settlements are **competent but locally specialized.**

- One village knows its well, road, bell code, and local husk patterns.
- Another knows different rules.

The player's role is **not** to teach survival from above. They become a
**carrier between competent islands**: comparing protocols, moving knowledge,
recruiting specialists, helping build wider coordination. (Ties into the
[village/union system](../Mechanics/village-and-union-system.md).)
