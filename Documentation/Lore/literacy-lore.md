# Literacy — Lore & Narrative Role

> The narrative/world meaning of literacy. For the actual mechanic, see
> `../Mechanics/literacy-system.md`.

## Why literacy is thematically central

The world ended because the cleric **learned to read** the wrong tome and
released the sealed divinity (see `ancient-history.md`). The sword-girl became a
sword partly **because she was literate** and could verify the cleric's research
(see `sword-girl-backstory.md`).

> The world ended because someone learned how to read the wrong thing.

## The player starts completely illiterate

The player **cannot read** written notes, ledgers, gate notices, old warnings,
labels, manuscripts, or technical records. At first, writing is just marks. They
may recognize icons or social symbols, but not script.

## The written language is Chinese

Not fake elvish — **literal Chinese**. This works because:

- Characters can be learned **one by one**.
- Once learned, a character is **automatically recognized everywhere**.
- Compounds become **partially readable** before fully readable.
- The player doesn't level an abstract "literacy" stat — they **remember
  characters** (a lexicon).

## Who teaches which words (biased literacy)

Each source teaches words tied to its own domain. This means *which* words you
know first depends on *who* you learned from — and partial literacy is dangerous.

- **The sword** gives the first foothold, but biased toward her wound: blade,
  blood, name, hand, oath, sever, iron, revenge, body.
- **Gate captain:** gate, outside, wait, open, close, dusk, three.
- **Healer:** sickness, fever, blood, breath, sleep.
- **Corpse-burner:** death, ash, salt, bone, burn.
- **Watchmaker:** hour, bell, early, late, tune, gear.
- **Scribe:** grammar, negation, numbers, names, particles.
- **The hive** can also teach words — **dangerously**. It might teach "open"
  before "do not."

## Partial literacy is dangerous

Knowing "gate" and "third bell" but not "do not" can get people killed.

This is the narrative payoff of the mechanic: literacy is **survival, power, and
risk** all at once.
