# The Sword-Girl — Backstory & Arc

> The rusty sword the player carries IS a girl in sword form. This is her story.
> For her in-game systems (metrics, dialogue, combat), see `../Sword/`.

## Crucial fact: not the true Soul Sword

The rusty sword the player finds is **NOT** the true Soul Sword. It is a crude
**occult imitation** created later using a **misunderstood ritual**.

| True Soul Sword | Rusty imitation (the player's sword) |
| --- | --- |
| Born from willingness / consent | Born from coercion, deceit, bad interpretation |
| Can fly | Cannot fly |
| Can speak aloud to others | Speech is **private to the holder only** |
| Can cast / act as catalyst | Cannot cast like a catalyst |
| Mythically legitimate | Not legitimate |
| — | **Rusted** because it is not the divine original |

It can only transmit **waves, sensations, thoughts, pressure, and speech-like
communication** to the person holding it.

## Who she was

She came from a **noble house** that survived after the Silver Anarchy calmed.
The house was rich/powerful by remaining-world standards — possibly because it
controlled silver, walls, old manuscripts, scribes, a clock tower, a fortified
estate, or critical survival infrastructure.

- The hero had died only a few years earlier.
- Her **father** was under enormous pressure to help finish the plague war or
  protect what remained.
- The father hired a **cleric-scholar** to research a way to beat the remaining
  plague.

## The cleric

- Wanted to be the **next hero** and take credit.
- Did **not** find the hero's true sword. Instead found an occult tome / crude
  interpretation of the soul-sword rite.
- **Lied**, claiming he had found a way to recreate or recover the hero's weapon.

Characterization: the father is a **desperate patron, not cartoonishly evil**.
The cleric is the **ambitious interpreter with a hero complex**.

## The forced ritual

- The true rite required **willing** soul sacrifice. The cleric **misunderstood**
  this and used coercion.
- In this world nobody casually volunteers their soul; "noble sacrifice" is
  suspicious propaganda. With so few humans left, forced soul sacrifice is
  especially ugly.
- The father may have authorized using a "disposable" person under pressure: a
  condemned prisoner, servant, debtor, criminal, or exposed refugee. (Moral
  compromise without caricature.)

## How she became the sword

She was probably **not** the intended sacrifice at first. Stronger version:

- She was **educated and partially literate in the old script**, involved because
  she could help **read / verify** the research.
- She **discovered the cleric's deceit** or the ritual's real nature.
- During a crisis, the cleric **staged or exploited a plague breach / silver-
  boundary failure** to erase witnesses and force the ritual under emergency
  conditions.
- Her family/house died in the disaster.
- The cleric used **her** as the stabilizing soul / name-anchor — because of her
  literacy, identity, house-seal connection, or because she intervened at the
  wrong moment.

She became the sword.

## Her revenge — already partly done

Her vengeance is personal: the cleric killed/caused the death of her family,
destroyed her house, stole her future, and made her the **proof of his fake
heroism**.

As a sword, she eventually got one form of revenge: the cleric wielded her, but
she **refused to comply, misaligned, withheld**, and caused his death.

**So the cleric is already dead.** Her justice is not complete, but her direct
target is gone — which is why her deeper arc cannot be simple revenge.

## Her deeper ambitions

- Recover the truth.
- Expose the cleric's lie.
- Restore her name.
- Understand what happened.
- Find whether she can be released / restored / transformed.
- Prevent the soul-sword rite from being repeated.
- Complete her **father's original dream** — in a non-corrupt way.

Her father's dream was a **final answer to the plague**. The cleric corrupted it
into a hero-making weapon project. Her mature arc: take the dream back — not by
forging another forced soul sword, but by helping develop **severance, literacy,
protocols, infrastructure, and knowledge that make humans harder for the hive to
occupy.**

## Her dislocated age

Her body stopped aging when she became the sword.

- Calendar age, body age, emotional age, and endured time do **not** align.
- Older than the protagonist in some ways: she endured years as a sword and
  belongs to an older world.
- Younger / arrested in others: she lost normal life, freedom, body,
  relationships, adulthood.
- Guiding line: **her body froze; her grief aged; her freedom did not.**

## Years as a sword

Solitary existence: rust, cold, dirt, rain, insects, faint voices, being moved or
abandoned, sensory deprivation, no agency, no one hearing her.

She remembers noble politics, old script, and pre-collapse manners — but may
**misunderstand current survivor technocracy** and scavenged-object culture. She
may be **horrified** that luxury goods from her world are now survival junk.

## Her arc with the player (three stages)

1. **Early — Hands.** She wants the player to be her **hands**. She cannot turn
   pages, open doors, point at glyphs, choose where to go, hold a lantern,
   retrieve evidence, or confront people. The player is mobility and access. The
   relationship is transactional and harsh.
2. **Midgame — Witness.** Once the player learns words, they can read records,
   understand what happened, challenge lies, distinguish memory from propaganda.
   The player becomes more than a carrier.
3. **Later — Partner.** Research partner, survival partner, combat partner.
   Someone who continues the research, investigates whether there's a way out for
   her, improves her existence if there isn't, gives her peace if she chooses it,
   and helps complete her father's anti-plague dream without repeating the
   original sin.

### How care is measured

Not by simple affection points. By whether the player **makes her world bigger**:
can she communicate with others, access records, refuse upgrades, decide what
happens to her body, know the truth, shape anti-hive research, and become more
than a weapon?

## Possible route outcomes

- Exploited weapon
- Comforted but unresolved sword
- Research partnership
- Release / peace
- Restored body
- Sovereign sword by choice
- Mutual bond
- Completion of her father's dream through non-coercive anti-hive infrastructure

## Confusion with the hivemind (useful)

People find a talking mental object and assume it's hive-related, because the
hive is associated with shared cognition. They are **wrong, but plausibly wrong.**
She is offended and asserts individuality: *"I am not the choir." / "Do not call
me many. I am one."*
