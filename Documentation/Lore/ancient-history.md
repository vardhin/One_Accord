# Ancient History & The Sealed Divinity

> Baseline lore. Established but expandable.

## The divinities

There were ancient divinities — **~10 ascended souls** from the beginning of eons.
One of them was a hivemind/possession god.

The refinement: he is **not** merely "a hivemind god." He is a god whose
**selfhood can occupy all things**. He believes only his individuality is real,
and that everyone else is material or extension of him. He was too metaphysically
dangerous — he could overtake even *them*.

> **Important (corrected canon):** the other deities did **not** seal him, and they
> are **not** themselves sealed. They are **forgotten, alive, and deliberately
> abstaining** — direct interference risks the hive taking even one of them, which
> would end everything, so they lend power only **indirectly, through glyphs.** The
> full soul-economy that governs all of this lives in
> `the-deities-and-the-soul-economy.md`; read it for the metaphysics this doc only
> sketches.

This is the seed of the game's central theme: **possession vs. partnership** (see
`../genre-and-themes.md`).

## The accidental release

Much later, a **cleric obsessed with ancient scripts** discovered:

- an ancient forest, and
- a tome equivalent to a **Rosetta Stone** for ancient/divine script.

By decoding the tome, the cleric achieved breakthroughs in reading divine script
— and **accidentally released the sealed hivemind divinity**. The hivemind first
took over the cleric. The plague began.

This directly links **literacy** to the world's fall: *the world ended because
someone learned how to read the wrong thing.* (See `literacy-lore.md` and
`../Mechanics/literacy-system.md`.)

## The early plague horrors

Early plague was nearly unstoppable. The hivemind could take over:

- bodies, rocks, dirt, sludge, armor, weapons, and other matter,
- forming grotesque body-horror constructs.

Over time the force weakened / became more constrained but stayed terrifying.

## Silver, then anarchy

- People discovered **silver** hurt or resisted the hive.
- Defenses emerged: silver cages, markings, boundaries, silver-lined houses,
  silver armor, silver weapons. (Silver perma-kills **dumb husks** but backfires
  on Actives — see [kill resolution](../Mechanics/kill-resolution.md).)
- Then **silver scarcity** caused revolt, hoarding, violence, class conflict, and
  anarchy — the **Silver Anarchy**.

## Fire, then stabilization

- Later, people found some forms could be controlled or destroyed with **fire**.
- Defenses: moats, fire weapons, fire trenches, fortified settlements.
- The world slowly stabilized — but only **after humanity was mostly gone.**
