# Genre Identity & Themes

## Status — concluded: descriptor stack, no coined name

We deliberately **do not coin a single genre word.** The canonical public-facing
descriptor is a **stack of recognizable comparisons** so players grasp it instantly:

> **A top-down 2D action-RPG survival-sim** (Stardew Valley *shape* — not art
> style — a 2D RPG you live in) **with Soulslike combat and a living, LLM-driven
> cast.** Tone ranges widely: **dark and brutal like Dark Souls** in places,
> **colorful and fairy-tale** in others.
>
> Shorthand: *"a dark Stardew Valley where the NPCs actually talk back — and the
> enemy is one mind deciding where to look."*

Closest real-world genre handles (from research): **action-RPG life-sim /
dark survival-RPG** for the Stardew-shaped frame (cf. *Kingsgrave*, *Rune
Factory*, *Moonlighter*, *Sun Haven*), plus **emergent-narrative / LLM-NPC** for
the living cast. The coined-label candidates below are kept only as historical
brainstorm; **none is canonical.**

## How we got here

- Early candidate: **Bounded-witness RPG / Witness Ecology RPG.** Rejected as too
  narrow — it missed the plague war and village mobilization.
- The stronger framing centers on the five pillars (see `OVERVIEW.md`).

## "x-like" checklist

A game is in this genre if it has:

- Finite husk population (the living can no longer be infected — the seal holds;
  see `Mechanics/plague-and-infection.md`).
- Enemy is **one mind on a finite thread budget**, dynamically allocating
  attention across bodies (`Mechanics/hivemind-threads.md`).
- Clearing weak bodies forces the enemy to **merge threads** → higher
  active-intelligence density (your progress sharpens it).
- Surviving settlements can organize, resist, deny, collapse, or unify.
- Social/logistical progress matters as much as personal combat.
- The player has one bonded weapon with agency.
- Some kills require second-layer 1v1 soul duels where the weapon's inner body
  is playable.
- Endgame forces hive concentration.
- NPC knowledge is bounded by what they saw/heard/learned.
- Deep agents remember the world, not just the player.
- Preventive measures eventually make humans less usable by the hive.

## Central thematic axis: Possession vs. Partnership

- The hive **possesses** bodies.
- The sword does **not** want to be possessed.
- The player must not treat the sword as a mere tool.
- Upgrades risk possessing / rewriting the sword's identity.
- Romance can become possessiveness.
- The hive wants unity through **erasure**.
- The sword/player relationship can become unity **without** erasure.
- Combat asks: who controls the blade?
- Soul duels are battles for severance and selfhood.

## Convergence of goals

The player and sword have different *apparent* goals but the same *final* goal,
revealed over time. They agree on destination but may disagree on method. The
sword's ancient wound and the player's personal quest converge at the same
source.
