# Plague, the Seal & the Soft-Infected

> The defining inversion of the setting: **the living can no longer be infected.**
> The old hero **sealed** the Hivemind, so infection is **not** a transmission
> risk. What remains is rarer and stranger — the **soft-infected**, human
> backdoors the sealed hive can still reach through. This is what makes the game
> *not* a normal zombie-survival.

## The corrected model: no infection of the living

The plague is **history, not an active spread sim** (see
`../Regions/world-structure.md` — the famine already settled the population).

- Most former people are **already husks.**
- The surviving living **cannot be turned.** The seal holds. There is **no
  incubation, no bite-risk, no "don't get infected."**
- This is the deliberate twist on zombie survival: usually infection is the core
  fear; here it is **off the table**, which frees the dread to be about something
  else — scarcity, the thinness of population, and the unease below.

> Every named life is statistically enormous (`world-structure.md`) — not because
> infection might claim it, but because there is no slack and no new people coming.

## Soft-infected — the residue the seal left

The seal stopped *full* turning, but not cleanly. Some people are **soft-infected**:

- Day to day they **are themselves** — fully human: they garden, talk, remember,
  love.
- But they are a **backdoor.** The sealed hive can **take them over** (spend a
  thread on them — see `hivemind-threads.md`). While puppeted they act for the
  hive; **afterward they have no memory** of what they did. A blackout puppet.
- The seal prevents *permanent* turning, **not** these intermittent possessions.

This is the setting's quiet horror: not "don't get bitten," but **identity doubt.**
Is the person across the table fully a person? Are you sure of yourself? — *"No,
I'm not a human."*

### Detection is gated late

- **Only late-game Light glyphs can identify a soft-infected person**
  (`../Glyphs/glyph-system.md`). Light's in-world identity is partly *the thing
  that reveals the soft-infected.*
- Before Light, you **cannot reliably tell.** Early and mid game you live among
  them blind. The sword may *sense unease* via Seek Accord
  (`../Sword/voice-and-brow-touch.md`), but cannot *confirm.*
- The tools to know arrive too late to feel safe — that is the point.

## Quarantine → Checkup

Because turning is impossible, the old **three-day quarantine** (wait to see if
someone turns) is **pointless** and is replaced by the **Checkup**: a screening /
observation practice looking for the soft-infected.

- The **1–2 person capacity** (`world-structure.md`) is now an
  **observation/checkup room**, not a turning-cell.
- The checkup can watch and screen, but **cannot confirm** soft-infection until
  Light exists. It trades in suspicion, behavioral tells, and custom — not
  certainty. That uncertainty *is* the social tension.

## Survivor customs (reframed)

The plague-era social logic still shapes the world — but now as **inherited
custom and screening against the soft-infected**, not active anti-transmission:

- Travelers wait outside the gate for **three nights** (now a checkup/observation
  custom, not a turning-watch — still load-bearing for *Three Nights Outside the
  Gate*).
- Returning scouts sleep under watch; outsiders use separate wells; gates do not
  open at dusk; children kept from old roads and quiet husks.
- Bodies are **burned, salted, or severed** — still mechanically load-bearing for
  denying the will a vessel (see `kill-resolution.md`), since husks remain.
- **Bell codes** signal: breach, active sighting, body recovery — and now also a
  *suspected takeover* in progress.
- Silent husks are avoided, not trusted.

Draw the social texture from **older plagues, not COVID discourse**: quarantine,
cordons, lazarettos, gatekeeping, corpse handling, ritual response, fear,
scapegoating, public trust, secrecy, safety-vs-necessity tradeoffs — but read
through this world's specific fact that *people can't turn, yet some aren't wholly
themselves.*

## The hive's strategic questions (updated)

Because it cannot infect the living, the hive instead asks:

- Which **husks** should I pilot, and where do I spend threads
  (`hivemind-threads.md`)?
- Which **soft-infected** backdoors are worth taking over, and when?
- Which **survivor networks** should I destabilize?
- When should I **reveal** intelligence vs. stay as dumb husks?
- How do I **stop villages from learning** severance / Light-detection methods?
