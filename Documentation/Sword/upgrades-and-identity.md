# Sword Upgrades — Bodily & Identity Negotiation

> Upgrading the sword is NOT pure stat improvement. It is a negotiation over her
> body and identity. For the world infrastructure that enables upgrades (forge
> levels, blacksmith, etc.), see `../Mechanics/forge-and-upgrades.md`.

## Core conflict

Each upgrade changes **stats AND identity.**

- The **player** may want damage.
- The **sword** may want dignity, memory, beauty, revenge, bodily autonomy, or
  restoration.

This conflict is **central**.

## The loaded questions

Every upgrade forces a choice:

- Do you polish away the rust?
- Replace the cracked hilt?
- Reforge the blade with enemy matter?
- Inscribe your name?
- Restore what it once was?
- Evolve it into something new?
- Perform repair **without consent**?
- Respect its refusal?

## Trust mechanics around upgrades

- At **low trust**, the sword lets itself be swung but **badly.**
- At **medium trust**, it permits some techniques.
- At **high trust**, it **volunteers** power.
- If **trust is broken**, it may still work but withhold flourish, refuse
  finishers, dull itself, overcommit, misalign timing, or **protect itself
  rather than the player.**

This must **not** be random annoyance — it should be **rule-based and
understandable.**

## The relationship IS the build

The sword's relationship functions as the player's build. **Not** warrior / mage
/ rogue. **The build is the relationship.**

### Relationship/build paths

| Path | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Dominating bond** | High damage, forced obedience, unstable consequences |
| **Devotional bond** | Defensive/protective power, slower growth, stronger survival |
| **Romantic/possessive bond** | High synchronicity, jealousy, exclusive techniques, dependency |
| **Professional bond** | Efficient & stable, lower emotional volatility, maybe weaker deep powers |
| **Exploitative bond** | Rapid power but betrayal risk |
| **Mutual-healing bond** | Deeper phase access, but requires giving up conventional upgrades |

## Stats are not classic RPG stats

Instead of Strength / Dex / Int, the relevant axes are:

**trust, resentment, shame, hunger, autonomy, dependence, fear, bond depth,
corruption, attunement.**

*(This is the upgrade-lens vocabulary. "Attunement" here is the combat/metaphysical
compatibility stat — renamed from "resonance" to avoid clashing with the sword's
dialogue Resonance metric. See `../Mechanics/relationship-stats.md`.)*

(See `../Mechanics/relationship-stats.md` for the combat-side set and how they
move.)

## A note on rust

There are **two separate things** called "rust":

1. **Emotional rust** — she may **hate being polished** because rust is proof of
   her history; she may feel **violated** by repair without consent. (See
   `sword-as-npc.md`.)
2. **Functional rust** — rust blocking her **sensory channel** (the glow/detection
   upgrade). This is **simple maintenance, NOT tied to memory.** (See
   `glow-and-detection.md`.)

Keep these distinct so the detection upgrade doesn't get tangled in the consent
drama.
