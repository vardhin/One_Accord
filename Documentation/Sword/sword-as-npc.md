# The Sword as an NPC

> The sword is not an inventory item. It is an NPC whose body-slot is "weapon."
> For her backstory, see `../Lore/sword-girl-backstory.md`.

## Core principle

You do not equip a weapon. **You negotiate with one.**

The sword has its own story, goal, preferences, trauma, and standards. The player
has their own story and goal. At first, neither realizes their **final goals
converge** (see `../genre-and-themes.md`).

## Modular entity identity

The world treats entity identity as **modular**: some NPCs inhabit bodies, some
objects, some places, some hive bodies, maybe memories. The sword is the prime
example — a person whose body slot is "weapon."

## Mutual desperation

- The sword is the **only weapon** the player can truly afford / use / rely on.
- The player needs the sword.
- The sword needs the player for **mobility, restoration, revenge, access to the
  world, or completion of its own goal.**

The sword must be **convinced** to do the player's bidding — and it tries to
convince the player to do its bidding too. This is **not a shallow approval
system.** The sword has agency, standards, and consent.

## Not a pet affection meter

The sword has **independent standards** and knows other people. It can say things
like *"he is better than you,"* because it has met better. It may admire another
person's grip, discipline, courage, repair skill, or restraint.

## The player is one entity among many

The sword does **not** treat the player as special by default. It does not detect
"protagonist energy." To the sword, the player is one entity among many — they
become salient only because they generate more logs, interactions, consequences,
and overlap with the sword's goals.

The sword may know many former wielders, other fighters, blacksmiths, NPCs,
enemies, villages, and personal histories. It can **compare the player
unfavorably** to others.

> The player is not the subject of memory. The player is an entity inside memory.

## Emotional logic (real, not anime-tsundere flavor)

The sword can feel **jealousy** — including toward inn conversations or
romantic/social attention — but it must arise from real emotional logic:

- Abandonment fear.
- Replacement trauma.
- Bodily insecurity.
- Former wielders.
- Fear of being treated as an object.

It can still be funny, sharp, or tsundere-coded — but the emotion underneath must
be **real**.

### Possible sword attitudes

- Prefers **calm hands** over heroic bravery.
- Respects someone who **cleans blood properly.**
- **Hates reckless courage.**
- Likes someone because they **do not ask it to be beautiful.**
- **Despises** the player for needing praise.
- **Hates being polished** because rust is proof of its history. *(Note: this is
  an emotional attitude. The functional rust/detection upgrade is separate and
  NOT tied to memory — see `glow-and-detection.md`.)*
- Feels **violated** if repaired or upgraded without consent.
