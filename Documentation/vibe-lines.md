# Vibe Lines & Canonical Principles

Short lines that encode design intent. Useful for keeping decisions on-theme and
for in-game/marketing voice.

## Sword & relationship

- You do not equip a weapon. You negotiate with one.
- The sword is not your companion. The sword is your build.
- Outside world: the sword is your weapon. Soul dimension: you are the sword.
- The sword knows you through closeness. The hive knows you through surveillance.
- The sword is not always available. It is drawn into speech.
- Brow-Touch guarantees attention, not cooperation.
- The player can always request the sword's attention. They cannot always earn
  its answer.
- The sword is a guide because she knows. She helps because she trusts. She
  explains because she has Clarity. She continues because she has Resonance.
- You can choose violence. You cannot always choose her violence.
- The sword is the translation layer, not the key.
- Her body froze; her grief aged; her freedom did not.

## Identity & memory

- The player is not the subject of memory. The player is an entity inside memory.
- The player is not special by ontology. The player is special by log density.
- The world does not track you. The world remembers you through bodies that were
  present.
- Nothing knows everything. Everything remembers something.
- Named NPCs are rare because personhood is rare now.

## The hive & scaling

- The enemy does not scale because the game says so. It scales because you are
  removing places for its mind to hide.
- Early: There are too many of them.
- Mid: Why did that one know my name?
- Late: Every remaining one is looking.
- Endgame: There is no remaining distance between hive and body.

## Sword vs. hive (distinction)

- The sword wants partnership. The hive wants absorption.
- The sword becomes intimate. The hive becomes omnipresent.
- "I am not the choir." / "Do not call me many. I am one." (the sword asserting
  individuality against the hive).

## Literacy

- The world ended because someone learned how to read the wrong thing.
- Objects are common; maintenance knowledge is rare.
