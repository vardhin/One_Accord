# Digzarr — The Great Fallen City

> The map's missing answer to **"where did the thousands live?"** The lore keeps
> insisting the few-thousand era was real (`../../Lore/premise-and-the-one-accord.md`,
> `../world-structure.md`) — but every authored place is a band-scale settlement of
> dozens. Digzarr is the **one true city** the old world had: now a vast hive-side
> ruin, the place the plague **first reigned**, and the only source on the map of
> recovered **pre-collapse engineering** — Persian wheels, clockwork automations,
> crossbows, and the manuscripts that teach them. A dense hub on the hive side.

---

## What Digzarr is, in one line

The **dead metropolis** — where most of humanity once lived and where the hive's
**oldest, grotesque first-generation horrors still reign** over the ruins, sitting on
the buried engineering knowledge that could re-arm the living. You loot the corpse of
the old world's largest body to carry its tech back to the few who survived it.

## Why it must exist (the lore pressure)

The world had **a few thousand** people before the binding (`../world-structure.md`).
A manuscript-clockwork society of that size **had a city** — mills, pumps, geared
gates, archives, workshops, experimental steam (`../../Lore/setting-and-tech-level.md`).
The famine and the hive scattered the survivors into the band-scale islands the map
shows today, but the **place those thousands actually lived did not vanish — it was
abandoned to the hive.** Three pressures converge on it:

1. **Demographic.** Nothing on the map currently holds a city's worth of former life.
   Digzarr is where the "few thousand" figure is made physically legible.
2. **Horror.** `../../Lore/ancient-history.md` says the **early plague nearly
   reigned unchecked** — grotesque body-horror constructs of bodies, rock, sludge,
   armor, and weapons. Those first-generation horrors have **nowhere to be** in the
   current region set. A city full of the densest concentration of bodies is exactly
   where the hive was once *most comfortable* (`../../Mechanics/hivemind-threads.md`) —
   so the **oldest, deepest-merged horrors still nest there**, never cleared because
   no one ever had the strength to retake a whole city.
3. **Technology.** The world has a **forge axis** (Vendur basics → Teling mastery) and
   a **glyph axis** (deity-lent magic). It has **no source for mundane, secular
   engineering** — the manuscript-clockwork tech the setting is named for. Digzarr is
   that third axis: **recovered pre-collapse engineering**, taught to the living.

## The stalemate, locally

Digzarr is the **inverted Vendur.** Vendur is the smallest functioning society frozen
by *sufficiency* — too stable to change. Digzarr is the largest society frozen by
*death* — the war here is long over and the **hive won this square outright, a decade
ago.** It is not a contested front; it is **occupied territory the living wrote off.**
The stalemate is that the single richest deposit of survival-knowledge on the map sits
inside the one place the survivors are too few and too weak to ever take back. The
player arriving is the first force in a generation that can even *enter*, let alone
extract.

> Rosetta is where the world **ended**; Digzarr is where the world **lived, and then
> was eaten.** Rosetta is a research site; Digzarr is a civilization's corpse.

## Distinct mood

**Vertical, monumental, and wrong.** Where every other region is human-scaled — a
hillfort, a camp, a homestead — Digzarr is the only place that **dwarfs** the player:
multi-storey ruins, collapsed tower-blocks, a stopped great-clock, dry aqueduct lines,
choked mill-races, plazas big enough to lose a band in. After a whole game of
stillness in tiny settlements, the city's **scale is the horror** — proof that there
was so much more, and it is all dead.

The hive's long tenancy shows as **deep threshold-matter** (`../../Mechanics/hive-concentration.md`):
not the fresh wrongness of Arghanzza but **settled, ancient possession** — buildings
that have been the hive's body for ten years. Masonry that breathes, streets paved in
fused bodies, machinery the hive has grown *into*. The texture is **grandeur rotted
into anatomy.**

## Local survival/social problem

**There is no settlement here — and that is the problem.** Digzarr has no living
faction to negotiate with; it is pure hive ground. The "social" problem lives at the
**edges and in who else comes here:**

- **The Vengarz run sorties into it** (`../Vengarz/region-vengarz.md` already sends
  recovery teams toward Rosetta; the city is the richer, deadlier prize). Digzarr is a
  place the Vengarz **lost people in** — there are standing rules, no-go quarters, and
  grief attached to it. The player's access to deep Digzarr may route through Vengarz
  trust and Vengarz maps.
- **Scavenger pressure.** A dead city is the obvious lure for the desperate and the
  greedy. The living problem is **who else is in the ruins** — lone scavengers, a
  doomed expedition, someone who went in for a blueprint and didn't come out, the
  occasional fool who thinks the city can be *lived in.*
- **The temptation of the city.** A recurring social beat across the map: should the
  union try to **reclaim a piece of Digzarr** for its space and resources — and is that
  hubris that wakes what sleeps there? The city is a standing argument among the
  survivors about ambition vs. survival.

## Local plague/hive behavior — where the first horrors still reign

Digzarr is the **only region whose hive presence predates the seal's stabilization.**
This is its signature:

- **First-generation horrors.** The grotesque constructs from
  `../../Lore/ancient-history.md` — the ones that *reigned* when the plague was young
  and nearly unstoppable — **never left.** They are the **oldest merged threads**
  (`../../Mechanics/hivemind-threads.md`): commanders that have squatted the same
  bodies and the same district for a decade, deeper-merged and stranger than anything
  the player has faced, because the hive was never *squeezed* here — it has had this
  ground comfortably the whole time.
- **A city as one body.** The hive grew into the **infrastructure**: it can drive the
  city's own dead machinery — drop a portcullis, turn a mill-wheel into a thresher,
  levitate masonry (commander telekinesis) on a city scale. The environment is an
  extension of the enemy.
- **The bait risk.** Because the hive is *comfortable* here, fighting deep into Digzarr
  is the strongest possible draw on the **thread scheduler** — pushing inward squeezes
  and merges the city's horrors exactly as the core loop describes
  (`../../Mechanics/hivemind-threads.md`). Digzarr teaches the tragic loop at full
  scale: clearing it makes what remains *worse*. A player can over-fight here and wake
  a Named first-generation horror that was better left sleeping.

## The settlement/ruin — the city itself is the dungeon

Digzarr has no living settlement; **the ruin is the content.** Authored as
**districts**, not rooms — a city explored in pieces, returned to as the player grows:

- **The outer ruin / approach.** Collapsed edge-blocks and dry aqueduct lines; the
  shallow, survivable layer where scavengers and Vengarz sorties operate. The
  player's first taste.
- **The works quarter.** Mills, pump-houses, the experimental-steam shop, gear-yards —
  where the **engineering manuscripts and machines** are. The tech payload lives here
  (below).
- **The great archive / engineers' hall.** A pre-collapse repository of **mundane,
  secular knowledge** — distinct from Rosetta's *divine*-script research. This is the
  source of the **printing-press milestone** the setting flags as a strategic future
  tech (`../../Lore/setting-and-tech-level.md`) — Digzarr is where that blueprint could
  be found.
- **The deep core / the old quarters.** Where the **first-generation horrors reign.**
  Late-game ground; the densest threshold-matter on the map. What the Vengarz lost
  people to.

## Defense/logistics challenge

The challenge is **extraction, not defense** — Digzarr inverts the usual region puzzle:

- **You cannot hold it.** Nobody can garrison a hive city. The logistics problem is
  **get in, get the manuscript/machine, get out** — staged, repeatable raids that go
  deeper as the player strengthens, not a settlement to fortify.
- **The carry-out problem.** Recovered tech is **heavy, fragile knowledge** — a
  blueprint, a working mechanism, a manuscript. Getting it out of the deep city past
  the horrors is the gameplay. Losing the carrier loses the knowledge.
- **Heat as the meter.** The deeper the district, the higher the baseline concentration
  and the faster drift climbs (`../../Mechanics/hive-concentration.md`). Digzarr is the
  region where the player most directly feels the scheduler deciding to *spend threads
  on them.*

## Sword-relevant emotional/history layer

The sword is from the **few-thousand era** (`../../Lore/premise-and-the-one-accord.md`)
— so Digzarr is the only region she might remember as a **living city**, not a ruin.
This is her counterpart to Vendur's homecoming, but colder:

- **Recognition of scale.** Vendur was her *home*; Digzarr is her *world* — the proof
  that the thin, settled stillness everywhere else is the **aftermath of something
  enormous.** She remembers the plazas full. The player walks her through the grave of
  the entire civilization she knew, not just her house.
- **The first horrors are her contemporaries' fate.** The grotesque things that reign
  here are what the plague did to the **people she lived among.** A heavier register
  than anywhere else — these were not monsters, they were Digzarr.
- **Tech vs. her knowledge.** Digzarr's value is **secular engineering** — the one kind
  of knowledge the sword is *not* the anchor for (she holds glyph/seal/old-script lore,
  not mill-craft). A quiet beat: the dead city knows things even she doesn't, and the
  player learns them from manuscripts, not from her. It de-centers her — usefully.

## The old-tech axis — Digzarr's mechanical reason to exist

Digzarr is the **source of recovered pre-collapse engineering**, a third tech axis
beside the forge (Vendur→Teling) and the glyphs (deity-lent). The model is the
**village & union system** (`../../Mechanics/village-and-union-system.md`): the player
**recovers a blueprint/manuscript in Digzarr, carries it to the right specialist in a
living settlement, that person learns it, and it becomes a buildable village/town
upgrade.** Digzarr is the **source**; the union loop is the **delivery.**

> Knowledge is barter, and **maintenance/operation knowledge is the rare thing**
> (`../../Lore/setting-and-tech-level.md`). A blueprint is worthless until the right
> hands learn to *work* it — so a recovered manuscript is a **quest to the right
> person**, not an instant unlock.

Example recoveries (final list deferred — see Open):

- **Persian wheels / water-lifting gear** → taught to a settlement with water/irrigation
  need; a union upgrade that eases a town's survival baseline.
- **Clockwork automations** → geared gates, automatic alarm-bells, mill/pump
  mechanisms — defensive and logistical union upgrades (auto-gates, bell-codes that
  ring themselves).
- **Crossbows** → a manufacturable ranged weapon line; taught to a smith/militia (a
  natural Training-Camp / Belgond militia upgrade, and a personal weapon for the
  player).
- **The printing press** (`../../Lore/setting-and-tech-level.md`) — flagged there as a
  **strategic future tech** for mass-copying quarantine rules, severance knowledge, and
  literacy primers. Digzarr's great archive is its natural source; a late, high-value
  union milestone.

This makes Digzarr **returnable and dense by activity**: the player comes back as they
grow strong enough for deeper districts, each run yielding the next blueprint that
re-arms the living map.

## Role on the spine

- **A hive-side dense hub** — the across-the-water counterpart to Vendur's human-side
  density. It is dense because the **city** is dense (like Rosetta), not because people
  live there (`../region-set.md`: stalemate framing — arriving late to a finished
  history).
- **Late-side ground.** Reached from the hive-side spine; deep Digzarr is endgame-grade.
  Its first-generation horrors and the printing-press milestone are payoffs the player
  earns over many returns, paralleling how Teling/Velduri deepen the human side.
- **Feeds the union, not a settlement to unite.** Unlike every other dense region,
  Digzarr has no people to convince — it **supplies the upgrades** the player installs
  *elsewhere*, making it the engine behind the union system's mundane-tech tier.
- **Map position:** across the water on the **hive side** — placement relative to the
  Arghanzza → Vengarz → Rosetta → Silent Core spine resolved in `connectivity-map.md`.
  Natural ties: the **Vengarz** run sorties toward it; it sits in the orbit of the old
  research/horror ground, not the human pocket.
