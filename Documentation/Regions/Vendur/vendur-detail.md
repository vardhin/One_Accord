# Vendur — Full Region Design

> The first **fully-authored** region. Builds on `region-vendur.md` (the overview —
> what it was, the horror, the room underneath) with the things a buildable region
> needs: **who lives there now, its physical shape, who holds which function, the
> dungeon, and the sword's homecoming arc as playable beats.** Vendur is the map's
> central dense hub and the place the sword's knowledge-quest sends the player first
> (`../region-set.md`, `../../Lore/premise-and-the-one-accord.md`).

---

## What Vendur is, in one line

A **diminished trade hub built on the sword-girl's murdered home** — the one
relatively warm, crowded, defensible place on the map, where the player gets the full
forge and silver supply *while* walking the sword through her own grave. It is a
**town on top of a wound** (`region-vendur.md`).

## Population: 13 households, band-scale

Vendur is **dense by this world's standards, which still means small.** It is one of
the highest-population places on the map and that is **~13 adult trader households** —
roughly **55–60 souls** total: ~13 working adults, most with a spouse, two or three
children each, and **4 elderly.** Everyone knows everyone. This is band sociology
(`../world-structure.md`: ~400 alive, no slack), not a city — so Vendur has **no
institutions, no guilds, no factions in the political sense.** It has **families, and
functions held by specific named people.**

> "Dense" here = the map's *crowded* place, not a crowd. Thirteen front doors feels
> like a metropolis after the stillness of everywhere else.

## The stalemate, locally

Vendur is the clearest expression of the **stalemate frame** (`../../Lore/premise-and-the-one-accord.md`):
it is the closest thing to a *functioning* society left, and it is **completely stuck.**
The silver boundaries hold, trade happens, nobody starves — and it **cannot grow.** It
can't expand its walls (the husk owns everything past them), can't take in many more
mouths (no slack — `../world-structure.md`).

The stalemate here is **not faction deadlock — it is no-slack paralysis.** Everyone is a
trader; everyone's interest is the same (keep the hub alive, keep the silver lines up,
keep the caravans coming), so there is no opposition to overcome — only **a community
with no one to spare and no redundancy.** You can't send a scout: that's one of thirteen
adults. If the one who keeps the silver dies, the knowledge may die with them. Vendur is
**prosperous by the standards of a dead world and frozen solid by its own sufficiency.**
The player arriving with the sword is the **first new variable in a generation.**

## Who lives in Vendur now: one faction, held by people

Squatters reclaimed a dead noble estate (`region-vendur.md`). There is **one social
body: the traders** — the ~13 households that make Vendur a hub. There are no rival
blocs. What looks like "politics" is **band-scale**: a handful of named people who each
*are* a function, and the loose pulls between households and generations.

### The traders — the one faction

The merchants, brokers, and caravan families who move seed-varieties, tools, medicine,
and maintenance knowledge between the survivor islands (`region-vendur.md`). Pragmatic,
rootless, **the people who renovated over the old house seal without knowing what it
meant** — the **collapsed-object-meaning theme** turned on the sword's family. They are
not indifferent out of cruelty; they simply *live here now.*

### Functions, not factions

Power in Vendur is **embodied, not titled** — held by specific bodies, not offices:

- **The silver-keeper.** One household holds the **silver stores and the boundary
  upkeep.** Because silver is *quiet* survival (`../../Mechanics/kill-resolution.md`),
  whoever maintains the lines effectively controls who lives — the nearest thing to
  authority Vendur has. Conservative by necessity (the boundary cannot be risked), and
  the loudest voice that the **talking sword is a hive vector** — the natural source of
  the "shove the sword onto a stranger" handoff suspicion
  (`../../Player/player-premise.md`). *Not a faction — one indispensable family everyone
  depends on.*
- **The remembering elders.** Some of the **4 elderly** are the only people with any
  memory of what Vendur *was* — half-superstitious, the ones who avoid **"the bad room"**
  (the cleric's chamber, `region-vendur.md`). They hold **memory, old keys, and access to
  the avoided places**, and they are the **sword's only living link** to her past. Their
  quiet grievance (strangers hung washing in the solar) is **generational, not
  factional**: the old who remember vs. the traders who built a life over the past and
  don't want to hear it.

> The quest engine is **not** a three-way lock to break. It's a small, warm, ordinary
> trader town that **cannot change itself** — and one stranger, carrying the one mind
> that remembers what this place was, is the first thing that can move it.

## The shape: a hillfort of two coupled nodes

Thirteen households can't be one blob behind a wall. Vendur is **two settlements masked
as one** — an old-English / Iron-Age **hillfort**: piled-**earth ramparts** topped with
**wooden palisade and pikes** (dump-construction, buildable by survivors with no masons
— right for a manuscript-clockwork society). The **silver line runs the rampart.** The
two halves are **mechanically distinct nodes that share one identity:**

### Upper — the hillfort proper

The defensible core on the high ground: the old estate bones — the **clock tower, silver
vault, the full forge, and "the bad room" beneath.** Holds the value and the
people-you-can't-lose. **Lower heat, harder to reach.** When the lower town is pressed,
survivors fall back *up* here.

### Lower — the trader town

The bulk of the 13 households live **down the slope, outside the inner ring** — the
**market floor, caravan yard, daily life.** This is the **warm, crowded relief** the
overview promises (`region-vendur.md`) — *and it is the exposed half,* outside the
strongest wall. **Warmer heat band, its own gating; the lower town can fall while the
fort holds.** That siege logic is band-scale and real: lose the lower town and Vendur may
drop below viability and have to merge or die (`../world-structure.md`).

### The edge households & the watch-clock tower

- **~4 of the 13 households sit on the outskirts as the guarding ring** — not a militia,
  not a faction: *the families whose position and job is the dangerous edge.* They hold
  the approaches, the gate, the outer pikes. Band-scale defense is *who lives on the
  exposed side*, not a standing force.
- **The clock tower doubles as the watch tower.** One landmark, two functions —
  timekeeping/records *and* the eye over the approaches and the lower town. The thing
  watching for husks is **her father's clock.** This binds the sword's recognition beat
  (the tower is *hers*) directly to the town's survival function.

### Vendur cannot defend or renew itself alone

A town of **traders, not soldiers** — its only defense is the 4 edge-households and the
tower. So Vendur **depends on a caravan tie to a separate region that trains and supplies
fighters**: the **militia / coming-of-age settlement** (`../region-set.md`) where
Vendur's children are sent to mature — teens leave soft and come back hardened adults, or
become militia, or don't come back. Vendur sends people and trade goods; it gets back
trained adults and defense. **A finished Vendur implies that neighbor** — see the spine
(`../connectivity-map.md`).

## The dungeon: the cleric's room beneath the town

The **sealed research chamber** where she was made (`region-vendur.md`) is Vendur's
dungeon and the **payload of the homecoming questline:**

- It is **walled off / superstitiously avoided** ("the bad room"): the **remembering
  elders** know where it is, the **silver-keeper** fears opening it (anything that
  disturbs the boundary is a threat), and most of the trader households simply don't
  think about it.
- It holds the **cleric's research, the real nature of the rite, and the evidence to
  restore her name** — the things her main arc has been chasing
  (`../../Lore/sword-girl-backstory.md`).
- In light of the new soul-economy (`../../Lore/the-deities-and-the-soul-economy.md`),
  the room can also seed the bigger reveal: the cleric was groping at **soul-binding**
  and the **deity-lent nature of glyphs** without understanding it — a first thread of
  "glyphs are gods" that the underground sanctum (`../region-set.md` #4) later pays off.
- **It is hot.** A place where a soul was coerced into a blade is a place the hive can
  press on — concentration spikes here (`../../Mechanics/hive-concentration.md`), and a
  **soul-duel** (`../../Sword/soul-duels.md`) is a plausible climax for the descent.

## The sword's homecoming arc (playable beats)

Vendur is where her arc **gears from Witness → Partner** (`../../Lore/sword-girl-backstory.md`).
Authored as beats, not a cutscene:

1. **Recognition under noise.** She realizes *where she is* amid the bustle — the clock
   tower is hers, the seal over the gate is her house mark turned trade logo. The
   horror is **personal and quiet** (`region-vendur.md`): *"They hung washing in my
   mother's solar."* (Drives Resonance/Trust beats — `../../Sword/`.)
2. **The strangers who mean no harm.** The traders' easy indifference and the
   silver-keeper's suspicion of the talking sword wound her differently. The player
   choosing how to move through this town *for her* is the first real Partner act.
3. **The elders' door.** Reaching the old people who remember — and through them, the
   keys to "the bad room." Earns the descent.
4. **The descent / the truth.** The dungeon: the cleric's research, the evidence, the
   soul-binding seed. The truth of her making, finally hers.
5. **What she does with it.** Restore her name publicly? Keep it? This is where the
   **player makes her world bigger** (`../../Lore/sword-girl-backstory.md`) — or doesn't.

## Buildings & facilities

Vendur is dense, so it carries the **full facility set** (`../../Npcs/system-npc-roles.md`),
upgraded past the starter settlement's bare versions:

| Facility | Vendur version | Where / who holds it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Forge** | The **full forge** (L0→4 chain, `../../Mechanics/forge-and-upgrades.md`) — the map's real blacksmith | Upper; the smith household |
| **Silver vault & boundary** | The estate's working silver lines + store, run along the rampart | Upper; the silver-keeper household |
| **Gatehouse** | Hardened access control + quarantine (1–2 capacity, `../world-structure.md`) | The line between upper & lower; the edge households |
| **Market / storehouse** | The barter floor; allocation of irreplaceables | Lower town; the traders |
| **Archive / decoder** | Old manuscripts, scribes, the clock-tower records | Upper; the remembering elders |
| **Infirmary** | Healer + plague-watcher / Checkup | Lower town; shared |
| **The clock / watch tower** | Diegetic landmark; hers; the eye over the approaches; possible bell-code comms node | Upper; the elders' memory |
| **"The bad room"** | The sealed dungeon (above) | Beneath the upper fort; avoided by all |

> **Threshold-matter look** if driven hot (`../../Mechanics/hive-concentration.md`):
> **tarnished silver and stopped clockwork** — possessed locks, halted gears, the clock
> tower going wrong. Vendur over-fought becomes a town of frozen, breathing machinery.

## Role on the spine

- **Mid-spine dense hub** (`../connectivity-map.md`): a power spike (full forge + silver)
  and the sword's emotional pivot, reached through at least one corridor from the starter
  settlement. The player carries her **home without knowing it.**
- **Two coupled nodes, one identity:** the upper hillfort (cooler, holds the value) and
  the lower trader town (warmer, exposed) are mechanically distinct — the lower can fall
  while the fort holds (`../connectivity-map.md`).
- **The handoff happens elsewhere** (`region-vendur.md`); the player *brings* her back
  over the rust-years' drift.
- Branch point: from Vendur the spine continues toward the hotter lore regions (Rosetta
  ruins → sanctum → mountain); a **caravan tie** runs to the militia/coming-of-age
  settlement (`../region-set.md`).

## Open

- **Named NPCs** among the 13 households — who is the silver-keeper, the smith, the
  remembering elders, the edge-household heads — **deferred to per-NPC authoring** under
  the 40-cap roster (`../../Npcs/`). The *functions* are pinned; the names are not.
- The **dungeon layout** and exactly how much of the soul-economy reveal lands here vs.
  is reserved for the underground sanctum.
- The **militia / coming-of-age settlement** at the other end of Vendur's caravan tie —
  registered as a region (`../region-set.md`) but **unnamed and not yet authored.**
- Exact **heat split** between the upper and lower nodes (resolve in the concentration
  pass, `../../Mechanics/hive-concentration.md`).
- A **place name** for Vendur in the world's invented-name style (e.g. *Ionir / Ira /
  Adry* flavor), written in the **glyph script** — a naming/script decision, not a
  conceptual gap.
