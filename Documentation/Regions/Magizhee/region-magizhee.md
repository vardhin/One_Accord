# Magizhee — Starter Settlement & the Breadbasket

> The first breathable pocket of civilization — **and the world's farm-dense region.**
> Small, legible, tutorial-rich, political, systemic. Scale inspiration: Goodsprings
> (Fallout: New Vegas); **economic identity: the breadbasket** — you start the game
> where the food comes from (a Stardew-true opening).
>
> **Current map placement:** Magizhee is the **hub** at the center of the human
> pocket, diagonal-opposite the Rosetta region across the water. Four early
> directions radiate from it: **north → the husk-held Old Dock** (the ferry
> crossing), **west → predator-mountains** (toward Vaelp, the Sword Sanctum), **east →
> Naerchu (the Training Camp)**, **south → Vendur**. East and south are the safe early routes.

## Economic identity: the world's grain breadbasket

Magizhee is the **one place with arable scale**, and the map's food system depends on
it (`../../Mechanics/demographic-and-food-system.md`). It works **26 farmers (13
farming couples)** and grows the **staple-grain surplus** that feeds the rest of the
map — especially **Naerchu** (the ~50-person training/mating camp that grows nothing),
via the **ownerless rotating food caravan.** Belgond supplies the protein/forage;
Magizhee supplies the grain. This makes the starter settlement the **agrarian heart of
the world economy**, not a generic crossroads — and ties the player, from the first
hour, to the food backbone the whole map hangs on.

## Census (~67 present; conserves into the world table)

Derived top-down (`../../Mechanics/demographic-and-food-system.md` §5–6). Magizhee is a
**farming-couple town** with a thin specialist layer and a work-militia ring:

| Group | Count | Role |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| **Farming couples** | **26** (13 couples) | The breadbasket core — work the grain terraces communally. |
| **Specialists** | **3** | A **healer**, a **blacksmith**, a **silversmith**. **Lightly partnered** — *some have a wife + occasionally an elderly parent; some stay bachelors* (partnering a specialist is rare). No glyph master (that's Naerchu + the hermit). |
| **Elderly** | **7** | Cook, help farm, and **mind the under-6 kids**. |
| **Work-cum-militia guards** | **18** (9 on shift) | Defend the farm + the hub; **work the fields off-shift** (`../../Mechanics/demographic-and-food-system.md` §4). |
| **Specialist partners** | **~3** | The rare specialist wives / elderly parents. |
| **Children (<6)** | **~10** | At home with the elderly; sent to Naerchu at 6. |
| **Present total** | **~67** | Plus **~15 of Magizhee's children away at Naerchu** (the largest share of its 34 trainees — the breadbasket reproduces most). |

> Magizhee's tutorial **Core Five** map onto this census: the **guard captain** (a
> work-militia lead), **healer**, **blacksmith** (the silversmith covers Magizhee's
> share of silver work), **trainer** (a senior work-militia veteran), and
> **quartermaster** (a farming-couple elder who runs grain allocation + the rotating
> caravan loading). The roles are pinned; **names deferred** (`../../Npcs/`).

## Scale & tone

Heavy inspiration from **Fallout: New Vegas / Fallout 3.** The first settlement is
**Goodsprings-scale:** small, legible, tutorial-rich, political, and systemic. Not
a giant city.

A **basic safe settlement** with:

- A **three-day quarantine rule** (the tutorial — see
  `../Player/opening-and-tutorial.md`).
- Crude defenses.
- Around **5 core NPCs** (see `../Npcs/starter-npcs.md`).

## Layout

- Common room / ration hall.
- Corpse pit / ash trench.

## World-position role

Magizhee is the root of the playable geography and the **hub** of the human pocket
— a small, constrained crossroads with four directions:

- **East → Naerchu (the Training Camp)** — a safe early route.
- **South → Vendur** (the Vendur Militia first, then the Hill Fort Market beyond).
- **North → the Old Dock** — naturally denied early: the ferry crossing is
  **husk-infested**, so going straight north throws the player at very tough husks.
- **West → predator-mountains** — naturally denied early: dangerous big cats and
  bears, on the terrain that climbs toward the Sword Sanctum.

This keeps the first map simple without making the world feel fake. The player
can choose militia-first or Vendur-first; both routes naturally fold back toward
Vendur.

## The first conflict (Goodsprings-style)

A small local crisis that teaches the whole game. Possible crisis:

> A quarantined traveler is **accused of turning before the third night.**

Stakeholder tensions:

- **Guard** wants to burn early.
- **Healer** says it's unclear.
- **Quartermaster** worries about supplies.
- **Trainer** worries about defense.
- **Blacksmith** needs yard access / tools.
- **Sword** may **contest** the killing (Stillpoint — see
  `../Mechanics/stillpoint.md`).

This single crisis surfaces: mercy dilemmas, bounded knowledge, resource
allocation, the sword's agency, and quarantine procedure — the whole game in
miniature.

## Helping before the breach

The player can help each of the five NPCs first; those choices **change the
fight.** (Detailed in `../Npcs/starter-npcs.md`.)

## Upgrade ceiling

The first settlement should **NOT** unlock full upgrades — only **basic
maintenance**, especially the **grindstone → sharpening** chain. (Forge Level 0→1
only; see `../Mechanics/forge-and-upgrades.md`.)

## Early route convergence

If the player goes **east to Naerchu (the Training Camp)** first, the camp should send them to
**Vendur** for supplies, permission, repair material, grain, medicine, or some
other concrete support. If the player goes **south to Vendur** first, the sword
nudges them there and the first dungeon can open from Vendur instead.

Arghanzza is no longer an early dependency. It sits on the far side of the river,
near the first right-side crossing, and belongs to the post-crossing pressure
ramp.
